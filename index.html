Travel, Transport and Tourism · Walking and Active Travel

Australia's favourite activity doesn't need a membership, a team, or a booking.

By Eebel Tan · Published 25 October 2026

Half of all Australian adults went walking for exercise or leisure in the past year, more than took part in any sport or gym activity.

For readers in Malaysia, where cities such as Kuala Lumpur are working to become more walkable, Australia offers a useful comparison. This story moves from the national picture down to Melbourne's footpaths: who walks, where, when, and why it matters.

02

Who walks, and for what

The older Australians get, the more they walk for leisure, peaking at 66% of 55–64 year olds. Walking to work is a different habit: fewer than 4 in 100 workers walk to work, at every age.

03

Where people walk: the national picture

Only about 2.5% of Australian workers walk to work, but the share rises where homes and jobs sit close together. The Northern Territory leads the states; in Victoria, the city centre and small country towns lead the regions. Victoria is divided into 66 statistical regions set by the Australian Bureau of Statistics, each about the size of a group of suburbs or a country district.

05

Why it matters: walking or driving

Where more people walk to work, fewer drive, so walking replaces car trips and the traffic and emissions that come with them. The Census was taken during COVID lockdowns, so commuting figures for New South Wales, Victoria and the Australian Capital Territory are lower than usual.

Scatter plot

Where people walk to work, they drive less: in Melbourne City, 1 in 4 commuters walk

Each dot is one of Victoria's 66 statistical regions, coloured by region type. It compares the share of commuters who walked with the share who drove; people who worked at home or did not go to work are excluded. The dashed line shows the overall trend: regions with more driving have less walking.

Source: Australian Bureau of Statistics, Census 2021, General Community Profile (G62)

06

The takeaway

Walking is already Australia's favourite activity, but mostly for leisure rather than for getting to work. The places where people do walk to work, compact city centres and small towns, show what more walkable cities could look like, including in Malaysia.
