Walking Australia

The country's favourite activity needs no membership, team or booking.

By Eebel Tan · Published 25 October 2026

Half of Australian adults walked for exercise or leisure last year, more than any sport or gym activity.

In Malaysia, Kuala Lumpur City Hall aims to raise walking and cycling from 18% to 40% of trips to work or school by 2028. This story moves from Australia's national picture to Melbourne's footpaths: who walks, where, when, and why.

Who walks, and for what

Leisure walking rises with age, peaking at 66% of 55–64 year olds. Walking to work stays rare: under 4 in 100 workers.

03

Where people walk: the national picture

Only about 2.5% of Australian workers walk to work, but more where homes and jobs sit close together. The Northern Territory leads the states; in Victoria, the city centre and small country towns lead. Victoria has 66 statistical regions, each about the size of a few suburbs.

05

Why it matters: walking or driving

Regions where more people walk to work have fewer people driving: less traffic and fewer emissions. Census day fell in COVID-19 lockdowns, so commuting in New South Wales, Victoria and the Australian Capital Territory is lower than usual.

Scatter plot

Where more people walk to work, fewer drive: in Melbourne City, 1 in 4 commuters walked

Each dot is one of Victoria's 66 statistical regions, coloured by region type. It compares commuters who walked with those who drove; people working at home are excluded. The dashed line shows the trend: more driving, less walking.

Source: Australian Bureau of Statistics, Census 2021, General Community Profile (G62)

06

The takeaway

Walking is Australia's favourite activity, but mostly for leisure, not commuting. Compact city centres and small towns show what walkable cities look like, with lessons for places such as Kuala Lumpur.
